# Hosts
Refers to devices assigned a number for communication purposes; this number identifies the host within a particular network (IP ADDRESS).

# Peer-to-Peer (P2P)
A host connected directly to another host, considered a topology in networking.
*Pros*:
- Easy configuration
- Less complex
- Lower cost
- Can be used for simple tasks like file sharing or printer sharing
*Cons*:
- No centralized administration
- Not very secure
- Not scalable
- All devices act as both clients and servers, degrading overall performance

##### ISP
What is an ISP?  
An organization that allows individuals and businesses to connect to the Internet.

### End Device
Initiates communication; uses the destination device's address to specify where to send the message.

### Intermediate Device
Connects end devices to the network, provides connectivity, and ensures data flows across the network.
*Functions*:
- Regenerate and retransmit communication signals
- Maintain information about existing routes within the network and internetwork
- Notify other devices of errors and communication failures
- Direct data along alternative pathways when there is a link failure
- Classify and direct messages according to priorities
- **Permit or deny the flow of data based on security configurations**

## Network Media
Modern networks primarily use 3 types of media to interconnect devices:
- Metallic wires within cables - **data is encoded into electrical impulses**
- Glass or plastic fibers within cables (fiber optic cables) - **data is encoded into pulses of light**
- Wireless transmission - **data is encoded via manipulation of specific electromagnetic frequencies**

*Criteria when choosing network media*:
- What is the maximum distance the medium can carry a signal successfully?
- What environment will the media be installed in?
- What amount of data and at what speed must it be transmitted?
- What is the cost of the medium and its installation?

#### Other Definitions
- **Network Interface Card (NIC)**: A NIC physically connects the end device to the network.
- **Physical Port**: A connector on a network device where the media connects to an end device or another network device.
- **Interface**: Specialized ports on a network device that connect to individual networks. Because routers interconnect networks, the ports on a router are referred to as network interfaces.

## Common Types of Networks
- Networks of different sizes:
  - **SOHO (Small Office and Home Office)**: Allows people to work from home or a remote office.
  - **Internet**: The largest network in existence, meaning "network of networks", an interconnection of **private and public networks**.
- **Small Home Networks**: Connect a few computers to each other and the internet.
- **Medium to Large Networks**: Used by corporations and schools, spanning many locations with hundreds or thousands of interconnected hosts.
- **Worldwide Networks**: Connect hundreds of millions of computers across the globe.

### LANs and WANs
- **LAN (Local Area Network)**: Provides access to users and end devices across a **small geographical area**.
- **WAN (Wide Area Network)**: Provides access to other networks over a **wide geographical area**, generally owned and managed by telecommunication service providers.

## Intranet and Extranet
- **Intranet**: Private connection of LANs and WANs belonging to an organization, accessible only by authorized employees or members.
- **Extranet**: Used by organizations to provide secure, controlled access to individuals who work for another organization but need data access.

## Internet Access Technologies
#### Home and Small Office
- **Cable**: Typically offered by cable television service providers; the data signal transmits over the same coaxial cable as television. High bandwidth, high availability.
- **[[DSL]]**: Digital Subscriber Line; provides high bandwidth and availability over telephone lines. Typically ADSL (asymmetrical DSL) where download speed is faster than upload speed.
- **Cellular**: Uses a cellular telephone network. Wherever there is cellular coverage, there is internet access.
- **Satellite**: Highly available in remote areas lacking terrestrial infrastructure. Requires clear line of sight to the satellite.
- **Dial-up telephone**: Inexpensive legacy option using an analog phone line and modem; very low bandwidth.
- **Fiber Optic**: High-speed light-based transmission.

#### Corporate Business
Requires higher bandwidth, dedicated connections, and managed services:
- **Dedicated Leased Line**: Dedicated copper or fiber circuits between offices for private voice or data networks.
- **Metro Ethernet**: Extends LAN technology into the WAN access layer.
- **Business DSL**: Includes symmetric options like SDSL (same upload and download speed).
- **Satellite**: Alternative when wireline options are unavailable.

### Network Architecture
Four core characteristics needed for a reliable user experience:
- **Fault Tolerance**: Limits the impact of a failure by providing redundant paths and graceful degradation.
- **Scalability**: Can expand rapidly to support new users and applications without degrading existing service performance.
- **Quality of Service (QoS)**: Prioritizes critical traffic (such as voice and live video) over less time-sensitive traffic during periods of congestion.
- **Security**: Protects physical devices and management access:
  - **Confidentiality**: Only authorized recipients can access and read data.
  - **Integrity**: Guarantees information has not been altered in transit from source to destination.
  - **Availability**: Assures timely and reliable access to data for authorized users.

#### Converged Networks
Designed to transport data, voice, and video over a single unified network infrastructure.

# Networking Trends
- **BYOD (Bring Your Own Device)**: Freedom to use personal laptops, tablets, and phones to connect to corporate networks.
- **Online Collaboration**: Real-time collaborative tools and platforms.
- **Video Communication**: High-definition video conferencing across distributed teams.
- **Cloud Computing**: Offloading data storage, backups, and applications to internet-hosted server environments.
  - **Public Clouds**: Available to the general public (free or pay-per-use).
  - **Private Clouds**: Dedicated entirely to a single organization.
  - **Hybrid Clouds**: Composition of two or more distinct clouds (public and private) bound together.
  - **Community Clouds**: Built for exclusive use by organizations with shared concerns (compliance, mission).

## Home Network Trends
- **Powerline Networking**: Uses existing AC electrical wiring to transmit data signals, providing connectivity where Wi-Fi or Cat6 drops are impractical.
- **Wireless Broadband (WISP)**: Connects subscribers to designated transmission towers using wireless technology, common in rural areas without cable or [[DSL]].

# Network Security Threats
- **Viruses, worms, and Trojan horses**: Malicious software executing on end devices.
- **Spyware and adware**: Secretly gathers user activity and tracking data.
- **Zero-day attacks**: Exploits executed on the day a vulnerability becomes known before a patch exists.
- **Threat actor attacks**: Deliberate cyberattacks against infrastructure.
- **Denial of Service (DoS)**: Attacks designed to saturate resources and crash services.
- **Data interception and theft**: Intercepting private business communications.
- **Identity theft**: Stealing credentials to obtain unauthorized access.

## Security Solutions
- **Antivirus and antispyware**: Endpoint protection against malicious payloads.
- **Firewall filtering**: Packet inspection blocking unauthorized ingress/egress.
- **Dedicated firewall appliances**: Stateful deep packet inspection.
- **Access Control Lists (ACL)**: Packet filtering based on IP addresses and port numbers.
- **Intrusion Prevention Systems (IPS)**: Real-time traffic analysis detecting active exploits.
- **Virtual Private Networks (VPN)**: Encrypted tunnels for secure remote access.
